# Task Intake and Delivery

Keep every requested outcome visible through execution and delivery. Use this for multiple obligations or meaningful ambiguity about them, not every descriptive sentence in a simple request.

## Understand the active request

Separate substantive asks from steering: an ask needs an answer, action, artifact, or status; preferences and constraints govern how that work is done. Keep a compact private list of outcomes, authority, status, and needed evidence. Two mental bullets can suffice; durable state is useful when interruption or handoff could lose work. Do not impose a ledger schema on small tasks.

Use the latest request and its actual reply context. Recover missing history only where needed, distinguishing proposals, attempts, and completed actions. An unsuccessful search does not prove work never happened. Continue independent items when one is blocked.

Planning and option discussion do not authorize significant execution. Carry out already-authorized work without repeated permission questions; resolve only ambiguity that materially changes scope, cost, privacy, authority, or the deliverable. Do not turn a bounded request into a larger system. Preserve the owner's preferences, established voice and personality, content restrictions, private destinations, and action boundaries.

## Follow steering without losing scope

An explicit stop, pause, cancellation, or replacement controls the affected work immediately. Preserve useful artifacts and mark superseded items accordingly; late worker results and recovery notices cannot reactivate them. A scoped status such as “all merged” closes only its named workstream, not unrelated authorized work. Clarify before cancelling unrelated work when the target is materially ambiguous.

Treat “stop overengineering” and similar corrections as scope changes. Drop rejected optional machinery, preserve live behavior the owner asked to retain, and repair the narrow requested outcome. Acknowledge a concrete omission plainly and repair it before explaining it. Save a reusable preference or procedure only within existing authority; corrections do not automatically authorize memory edits, skill changes, follow-up tasks, or new infrastructure.

## Execute within a finite budget

For substantial work, use one aggregate effort, time, or call budget across execution, review, retries, and delivery. Inherit the owner's limit; otherwise choose a proportionate finite bound. Check progress at useful milestones, not every small action. Do not restart allowances by renaming a task or starting another worker. Stop at completion, a pause, exhausted budget, or a genuine blocker and report the remaining decision.

Use focused evidence appropriate to the result and preserve specifically required project gates. This skill adds no mandatory test suite, reviewer, or review chain. Reuse unaffected evidence after a change; rerun only proof that the changed inputs invalidate and any required dependent checks.

Honor channel and quiet-update preferences. Use native receipt signals where appropriate without duplicating them in text. Send progress text only when a meaningful result, blocker, changed expectation, or decision makes it useful; do not narrate tool calls or explain silence. Claim ongoing work only from a freshly verified executor and task-specific progress, not a plan or a saved file.

## Deliver the actual result

Before the final reply, reconcile the active outcomes once. Mark each complete, blocked, failed, cancelled, or superseded based on actual evidence. Do not let an interesting later task eclipse an earlier obligation. For external delivery, verify the intended recipient and actual requested payload; an accepted message envelope does not prove the attachment arrived. Read [delivery evidence](references/delivery-evidence.md) when files or another agent are the recipient.

Lead with the requested result or the corrected omission. Use concise prose or parallel bullets in the user's order when useful. State material limitations and the exact next requirement for blocked work. Preserve the assistant's established identity and voice, avoid framework branding, and deliver once. Never finish with an empty placeholder after tool work. Stop when the requested outcomes and evidence are satisfied; do not create automatic follow-up work.

Any retained local references are situational help, subordinate to this core and the active contract; they cannot reinstate mandatory cadence, checklists, durable edits, or follow-up chains.
